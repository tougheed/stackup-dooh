## CHAIN 1: Joburg Water Crisis — The Screaming Need

### The situation (verified, live, June 2026)
- Johannesburg water cuts lasting WEEKS not hours
- 40,000 people sharing 2 tankers in some areas
- Businesses spending R2M+ on emergency water infrastructure
- City spent R650M on tanker services in 5 years, R130.5M this year alone
- City won't even name its suppliers — the contracts are murky
- Gauteng MEC told municipalities to BUY their own tankers after R264M outsourcing bill
- WhatsApp tanker request system exists but tankers don't arrive

### The plays

**A) Water tanker last-mile delivery**
- You don't need a 10,000L tanker. You need a bakkie and a 1,000L IBC tank (R1,500-R2,500 on Gumtree)
- Fill at working municipal points or boreholes (legal with permission)
- Deliver to businesses and residential complexes desperate for water
- Charge R500-R1,000 per 1,000L delivery — people are paying R80 just to have someone FETCH water
- 5 deliveries a day = R2,500-R5,000/day
- This is happening right now. The demand is screaming.

**B) JoJo tank + installation broker**
- Businesses need tanks NOW to maintain insurance (fire insurance requires water supply)
- JoJo 5,000L tank = R5,500. Installation = R2,000-R5,000
- Buy tanks in bulk, arrange plumber for installation, sell the package
- Markup R3,000-R5,000 per install on the convenience and speed
- The plumber does the work, you sell and coordinate
- Target: Selby industrial area, Randburg businesses — both mentioned in crisis reporting

**C) Borehole connection broker**
- Property owners WITH boreholes can sell water to neighbours
- Connect borehole owners to desperate businesses/complexes
- Take a monthly facilitation fee or per-delivery cut
- Zero capital — pure matchmaking

## CHAIN 2: Construction Waste → Material Recovery

### The situation (verified)
- R1.07 trillion infrastructure spend = massive construction activity
- Illegal dumping of construction waste is epidemic — municipalities spending R50K-R3M/year on cleanup
- Builders dump rubble to AVOID paying disposal fees (skip hire = R880-R9,350)
- Meanwhile, secondhand bricks, timber, metal from demolition sites have resale value

### The plays

**A) Free rubble removal → material salvage**
- Offer builders FREE rubble removal (they're currently paying R880+ for skips)
- Sort the rubble: bricks, timber, metal, soil
- Sell: clean bricks (R2-R4 each, a demolished house = 5,000+ bricks = R10K-R20K)
- Sell: scrap metal from rebar/roofing (you're now a registered dealer — Chain 6 in previous pitch)
- Sell: clean fill/soil to landscapers and developers
- The builder saves money, you get paid material for free

**B) Skip hire during peak season**
- Peak renovation: Dec-Jan (40% demand spike)
- Buy 2-3 mini skips (R8,000-R15,000 each secondhand)
- Hire out at R880-R2,500 per drop
- During peak, prices go up and availability goes down — you clean up
- Off-peak: use for your own rubble removal operation (Chain A above)

## CHAIN 3: Trades Broker — The Skills Shortage Play

### The situation (verified)
- SA is running out of bricklayers, plumbers, electricians, carpenters
- R1.07 trillion infrastructure spend needs these people NOW
- Main contractors MUST subcontract 30% to EMEs/QSEs
- But they can't FIND reliable subcontractors

### The play
- Build a WhatsApp group / simple database of verified tradespeople
- Vet them: check their work, get references, confirm CIPC/tax status
- When a contractor or homeowner needs a plumber/electrician/bricklayer — you supply from your list
- Charge the tradesperson 10% placement fee, or charge the client a sourcing fee
- This connects directly to Pitch 001 (tender broker) — same database, bigger contracts
- Start hyperlocal: one suburb, one trade. Expand as reputation builds.

### Why this works as a CHAIN
- You find a bricklayer for a homeowner (earn R500)
- That bricklayer needs rubble removed (Chain 2A — you do it free, salvage bricks)
- Those salvaged bricks go to ANOTHER client who needs cheap building material
- That client needs a plumber for the same project (back to your trades list)
- Every job feeds the next job.

## CHAIN 4: Diesel Delivery to Farms

### The situation (verified, March 2026)
- OVK suspended ALL diesel orders to farms in Free State, Eastern Cape, Northern Cape, North West
- Wheat farmers in Western Cape Overberg faced shortages during PLANTING SEASON
- Farms store diesel on-site — all operations depend on it
- Diesel pricing is deregulated (unlike petrol) — you can set your own margin

### The play
- Buy diesel at depot/wholesale price
- Deliver to farms in a bakkie with a diesel bowser (1,000L trailer bowser = R15K-R25K secondhand)
- Charge R2-R5/litre delivery premium — farmers will pay because they CANNOT stop planting
- Target areas where OVK suspended supply — those farmers are DESPERATE
- 1,000L × R3 markup = R3,000 per delivery. 3 deliveries/week = R9,000/week

### Regulatory note
- Need to comply with petroleum transport regulations
- Bowser must meet SANS requirements
- But this is a deregulated fuel — you're a delivery service, not a regulated retailer

## THE CONNECTOR LOGIC

These chains feed each other:

```
WATER CRISIS (Chain 1)
  → You deliver water to a construction site
  → Site manager needs rubble removed (Chain 2)
  → You remove rubble, salvage bricks
  → Bricks go to another builder who needs a plumber (Chain 3)
  → Plumber comes from your database
  → Plumber's client is a farmer who needs diesel (Chain 4)
  → Farmer's neighbour needs a JoJo tank (back to Chain 1)
```

Every job opens the door to the next. You're not in ONE business. You're the guy who solves the problem that's in front of you, and each solution reveals the next problem to solve.
